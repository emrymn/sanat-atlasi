The result is a tall vertical (1:3) bird's-eye storybook map of one art-historical period.

{{YERLESIM}}

Paint the whole picture edge to edge as one continuous landscape. Nothing of the flat sketch may remain: no blank paper background, no flat beige strip, no vignettes or framed scenes, no clouds separating the zones. The zones flow into each other.

The road is a real painted road whose surface changes with each zone (paved stone in Florence, a canal-side quay in the Flemish town, a snowy track in the village, a stone bridge in Venice), but its position and width never change.

Each of the 7 ovals becomes a small, round, empty paved plaza level with the road. Nothing stands on a plaza: no animals, objects or people (a small statue will be placed there later). The period structure named for that level stands right beside its plaza.

The world is made of famous Renaissance paintings merged into one land. Each hidden detail stays next to its own level and appears only once. Each zone is painted in the manner of its paintings, but the whole picture shares one consistent light and a gentle bird's-eye perspective.

From bottom to top:
1 (lowest plaza): Early Renaissance Florence. Beside the plaza, a Brunelleschi loggia with slender columns and round arches. Hidden nearby: Venus standing on a scallop shell at a small shore (Botticelli, The Birth of Venus), knights on horseback with broken lances lying on the ground (Uccello, The Battle of San Romano), the kneeling angel and the Virgin under a portico (Fra Angelico, The Annunciation).
2: A Flemish canal town. Beside the plaza, a stepped-gable canal house with an open window. Hidden nearby: oranges on a windowsill and a pair of wooden clogs at a doorway (Van Eyck, The Arnolfini Portrait), a hare sitting in the grass (Dürer, Young Hare).
3: A snowy Northern village with a small church beside the plaza. Hidden nearby: hunters with dogs coming down a hill and skaters on frozen ponds below (Bruegel, The Hunters in the Snow), a strange pink fountain and giant fruits (Bosch, The Garden of Earthly Delights).
4 (middle): The High Renaissance. Beside the plaza, Bramante's Tempietto, a small round temple with columns. Hidden nearby: a bridge and a winding path in misty blue-green hills (the landscape behind Leonardo's Mona Lisa), two hands reaching toward each other in a cloud (Michelangelo, The Creation of Adam), philosophers gathered under a great arch (Raphael, The School of Athens).
5: Venice. Beside the plaza, a stone bridge over a canal with a gondola. Hidden nearby: a soldier and a nursing woman by broken columns under lightning (Giorgione, The Tempest), a banquet on a colonnaded balcony (Veronese, The Wedding at Cana), Saint Francis before a rocky outcrop in golden light (Bellini, Saint Francis in the Desert).
6: Mannerism. Beside the plaza, a gateway with an elongated, stretched staircase. Hidden nearby: elongated pink and pale-blue figures carrying Christ (Pontormo, The Deposition), three sisters playing chess at a garden table (Sofonisba Anguissola, The Chess Game).
7 (top plaza): Beside the plaza, Giulio Romano's Palazzo Te. Hidden nearby: an unfinished row of tall columns (Parmigianino, Madonna with the Long Neck). Near the very top, a storm-lit hill town under a dramatic green-grey sky (El Greco, View of Toledo).

The top and bottom edges fade into soft mist so the map can join the neighbouring periods. No text, letters, numbers or watermark. No frames or borders. No modern objects. Small figures may appear inside the hidden scenes, but no large characters stand on the road.
