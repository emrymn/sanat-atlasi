Use the attached layout sketch as a strict composition guide. The result is a tall vertical (1:3) bird's-eye storybook map of one art-historical period. Keep the winding road EXACTLY where the beige road is in the sketch, with the same width and the same curves, entering at the bottom centre and leaving at the top centre. At each of the 7 oval spots on the road, the road surface must stay clear, flat and well lit (a small statue will be placed there later); paint one small period structure standing right beside the road at each oval.

Replace the flat sketch with a rich, painted landscape that captures the spirit of the Italian and Northern Renaissance. The world is made of famous Renaissance paintings merged into one continuous land: each zone is painted in the manner of its painting, but the whole picture shares one consistent light and a gentle bird's-eye perspective.

From bottom to top:
1 (lowest oval): Early Renaissance Florence. Beside the road, a Brunelleschi loggia with slender columns and round arches. Hidden nearby: a scallop shell on a small shore (Botticelli, The Birth of Venus), broken lances lying on the ground (Uccello, The Battle of San Romano), an angel under a portico (Fra Angelico, The Annunciation).
2: A Flemish canal town. Beside the road, a stepped-gable canal house with an open window. Hidden nearby: oranges on a windowsill and a pair of wooden clogs at a doorway (Van Eyck, The Arnolfini Portrait), a hare in the grass (Dürer, Young Hare).
3: A snowy Northern village with a small church beside the road. Hidden nearby: hunters with dogs coming down a hill and skaters on a frozen pond (Bruegel, The Hunters in the Snow), a strange pink fountain and giant fruits (Bosch, The Garden of Earthly Delights).
4 (middle): The High Renaissance. Beside the road, Bramante's Tempietto, a small round temple with columns. Hidden nearby: a bridge and a winding path in misty blue-green hills (the landscape behind Leonardo's Mona Lisa), two hands reaching toward each other in a cloud (Michelangelo, The Creation of Adam), philosophers gathered under a great arch (Raphael, The School of Athens).
5: Venice. Beside the road, a stone bridge over a canal with a gondola. Hidden nearby: lightning over broken columns (Giorgione, The Tempest), a banquet on a colonnaded balcony (Veronese, The Wedding at Cana), a rocky outcrop in golden light (Bellini, Saint Francis in the Desert).
6: Mannerism. Beside the road, a gateway with an elongated, stretched staircase. Hidden nearby: elongated pink and pale-blue figures (Pontormo, The Deposition), a chessboard on a garden table (Sofonisba Anguissola, The Chess Game).
7 (top oval): Beside the road, Giulio Romano's Palazzo Te. Hidden nearby: an unfinished row of tall columns (Parmigianino, Madonna with the Long Neck). Near the very top, a storm-lit hill town under a dramatic green-grey sky (El Greco, View of Toledo).

The top and bottom edges fade into soft mist so the map can join the neighbouring periods. No text, letters or numbers. No frames or borders. No modern objects. Small figures may appear inside the hidden scenes, but no large characters stand on the road.
