The result is a tall vertical (1:3) bird's-eye storybook map of one art-historical period.

Paint the whole picture edge to edge as one continuous landscape. Nothing of the flat sketch may remain: no blank paper background, no flat beige strip, no vignettes or framed scenes, no clouds separating the zones. The zones flow into each other.

The road is a real painted road whose surface changes with each zone (paved stone in Florence, a canal-side quay in the Flemish town, a snowy track in the village, a stone bridge in Venice), but its position and width never change.

Each of the 7 ovals becomes a small, round, empty paved plaza, flat and at ground level like the road (not a raised disc, step or pedestal). Nothing stands on a plaza: no animals, objects or people (a small statue will be placed there later). The period structure named for that level stands right beside its plaza.

The world is made of famous Renaissance paintings merged into one land. Each hidden detail stays next to its own level and appears only once. Each zone is painted in the manner of its paintings, but the whole picture shares one consistent light and a gentle bird's-eye perspective. Use a light, bright palette with clear daylight; the picture must not look dark or varnished. Keep it calm and uncluttered: each hidden detail is one small scene, and at least half of the picture is quiet open space (sky, water, meadows, paved squares, soft hills) with no figures or buildings. No crowds, no extra scenes, no extra buildings beyond those listed.

From bottom to top:
1 (lowest plaza): Early Renaissance Florence. Beside the plaza, a Brunelleschi loggia with slender columns and round arches. Hidden nearby: Dante in a red robe holding his open book, with the walls and Brunelleschi's dome of Florence beside him and the terraced mountain of Purgatory behind (Domenico di Michelino, Dante and the Three Kingdoms).
2: A Flemish canal town. Beside the plaza, a stepped-gable canal house with an open window. Hidden nearby: the Arnolfini couple in their room seen through the window, with oranges on the sill and wooden clogs at the door (Van Eyck, The Arnolfini Portrait).
3: A snowy Northern village with a small church beside the plaza. Hidden nearby: hunters with dogs coming down a hill and skaters on frozen ponds below (Bruegel, The Hunters in the Snow).
4 (middle): The High Renaissance. Beside the plaza, Bramante's Tempietto, a small round temple with columns. Hidden nearby: two hands reaching toward each other in a cloud (Michelangelo, The Creation of Adam).
5: Venice. Beside the plaza, a stone bridge over a canal with a gondola. Hidden nearby: a banquet on a colonnaded terrace (Veronese, The Wedding at Cana).
6: Mannerism. Beside the plaza, a gateway with an elongated, stretched staircase. Hidden nearby: three sisters playing chess at a garden table (Sofonisba Anguissola, The Chess Game).
7 (top plaza): Beside the plaza, Giulio Romano's Palazzo Te. Near the very top, a storm-lit hill town under a dramatic green-grey sky (El Greco, View of Toledo).

The top and bottom edges fade into soft mist so the map can join the neighbouring periods. No text, letters, numbers or watermark. No frames or borders. No modern objects. Small figures may appear inside the hidden scenes, but no large characters stand on the road.
