Image 1 is the bottom part of a tall bird's-eye storybook map painted as one bright, colourful painting: an Expressionist fjord with a swirling orange and red sky, blue horses grazing on a green hill, a wooden road winding up the middle, colourful wooden houses by the water on the right. Image 2 is Edvard Munch's painting The Scream (1893), for reference only. The mask opens only one area at the lower left; everything else is locked and must not change.

Repaint the open area as a faithful small version of Munch's The Scream, so that it is instantly recognisable as that painting: the long wooden bridge with its railing running diagonally from the lower left corner up and away to the left distance; in front, the pale, skull-like, hairless figure in a dark blue-black robe, holding both hands to its face, mouth open in a scream, its body curving like a wave; two small dark figures in hats walking away at the far end of the bridge. Behind the railing, the blue-violet fjord curving away and the blood-red, orange and yellow wavy sky, painted with Munch's long, flowing, wavy brushstrokes and his exact colours (blood red, orange, ochre, deep blue, violet). Seen from the same slightly raised angle as the rest of the map. It blends softly into the swirling sky, the green hill with the horses above it and the wooden road on its right.

No new buildings, no frame or panel: the scene is painted directly into the landscape. No text, letters, numbers, signature, watermark, frames or borders.
