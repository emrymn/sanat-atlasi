The result is a tall vertical (1:3) bird's-eye storybook map of one art-historical period: French Classicism and the late Italian Baroque, a journey from the gardens of Versailles up into a painted Roman sky.

{{YERLESIM}}

The picture has two zones from bottom to top, each about half of its height, with one plaza in the middle of each: France (bottom half) and Rome (top half). Paint the whole picture edge to edge as one continuous landscape. Nothing of the flat sketch may remain: no blank paper background, no flat beige strip, no vignettes or framed scenes. The zones flow into each other.

The road is a real painted road whose surface changes with each zone (pale raked gravel in the gardens of Versailles, warm travertine paving in Rome), but its position and width never change. It runs without any break from the bottom edge (entering at the bottom centre) to the top edge (leaving at the top centre); it never fades out, ends or disappears behind clouds.

Each of the {{N}} ovals becomes a small, round, empty paved plaza, flat and at ground level like the road (not a raised disc, step or pedestal). Nothing stands on a plaza: no fountain, basin, water, animals, objects or people (a small statue will be placed there later). The period structure named for that level stands right beside its plaza.

The style changes from bottom to top, as the period does:
- Bottom half (France): the calm, ordered ideal landscape of Nicolas Poussin and Claude Lorrain. Soft golden evening light, a hazy luminous horizon, feathery trees framing the view, clear geometry, serene balanced composition, clear cool blues and warm golds.
- Top half (Rome): a Baroque ceiling fresco in the manner of Andrea Pozzo and Giovanni Battista Gaulli. The sky opens upward into an illusionistic heaven: radiant golden light breaking through swirling clouds, small flying figures and putti in pastel robes, painted architecture rising into the sky in steep perspective.
The two styles blend softly where the zones meet; no hard border.

Use a light, luminous and lively palette with varied, clear colours: clear sky blue, fresh greens of lawns and trees, sparkling blue water in the garden fountains, cream and gold stone, rose and terracotta in Rome, pastel rose, gold and blue in the painted sky. The picture must not look dark, varnished or uniformly golden-brown, and must not be neon or oversaturated. The world is made of famous paintings merged into one land. Each hidden detail is a small scene painted directly into the landscape, never a framed picture, panel, canvas or rectangle pasted on the map, and appears only once. Keep each hidden scene small (no wider than about a third of the picture). At least half of the picture is quiet open space (sky, gardens, water, lawns, paved squares). No crowds, no extra scenes, no extra buildings beyond those listed.

From bottom to top:
1 (lower plaza): Versailles. Beside the plaza, the long golden-stone garden façade of the Palace of Versailles, and André Le Nôtre's formal gardens: geometric parterres of clipped box hedges, round fountains with jets, a long water canal leading to the horizon. Hidden nearby, in a quiet grove beside the gardens: four shepherds in classical robes gathered around a stone tomb, one kneeling and tracing the carved inscription with his finger (Poussin, Et in Arcadia Ego). Below the gardens, towards the bottom edge: a sunny pastoral valley in the manner of Claude Lorrain, with a small round classical temple, tall feathery trees and villagers dancing to music in the shade beside the road (Claude Lorrain, The Village Festival). No sea, no harbour and no ships anywhere.
2 (upper plaza): Rome. Beside the plaza, the façade and dome of the church of Sant'Ignazio, its painted ceiling opening into the sky above. Hidden nearby, beside a stone well in a small square: Rebecca in a white dress and blue skirt holding a copper jug, a bearded man in a red turban offering her a necklace, a camel and a cow behind them (Giovanni Battista Piazzetta, Rebecca and Eliezer at the Well; everyone fully clothed). In the open sky above: a swirl of angels in rose, gold and green robes carrying a figure up through the clouds towards the light (Sebastiano Ricci, Apotheosis of Saint Sebastian; all figures draped).

The top edge fades into soft pastel clouds and the bottom edge into soft green meadow so the map can join the neighbouring periods; the road reaches both edges. No text, letters, numbers or watermark. No frames or borders. No modern objects. Small figures may appear inside the hidden scenes, but no large characters stand on the road.
