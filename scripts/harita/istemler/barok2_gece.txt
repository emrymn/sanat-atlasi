Image 1 is a finished bird's-eye map of French Classicism and the late Italian Baroque, in daylight. Repaint exactly the same picture at night, as one of Louis XIV's famous night festivals at Versailles. Keep the composition identical: the road, both round plazas, every building, figure, tree, fountain and cloud stay in exactly the same place, size and shape; nothing is added, removed or moved except light. Keep the painting style (oil painting in the manner of the old masters).

Only the lighting changes:
- The sky becomes a deep night blue with stars; over the gardens of Versailles, a few golden fireworks burst softly in the sky, reflected in the long canal and the fountains.
- The palace of Versailles glows with hundreds of lit windows; torches and small lanterns line the garden paths and the parterres; the fountains sparkle with light.
- The Roman church and houses have warm candlelit windows; a few torches burn along the street.
- The painted ceiling sky at the top becomes a starry night heaven: the clouds lit from within by warm golden light, the angels glowing softly as if by candlelight.
- The pastoral valley at the bottom lies in soft blue moonlight; the dancing villagers are lit by a small bonfire and lanterns.
- The road is softly lit along its length by lanterns, and a lantern stands beside each round plaza so that both plazas lie in their own warm pool of light and stay clearly visible.

The round plazas stay empty: no fountain, water, figure or object on them. The flat grey margins on both sides (if any) continue the same night scenery. No text, letters, numbers, watermark, frames or borders.
