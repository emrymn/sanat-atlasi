Image 1 is a finished bird's-eye map of Impressionism in warm golden afternoon light: one river winding from Paris (bottom: the Gare Saint-Lazare, a garden café with dancers under lanterns, quays and sailing boats) through Giverny (middle: Monet's pink house, the water-lily pond with a green Japanese footbridge, poppy meadows, haystacks) to a Japanese water garden with blossoming plum trees, a tea house and a red pagoda (top). Repaint exactly the same picture at night. Keep the composition identical: the road, all three round plazas, every building, figure, tree, boat and bridge and the river stay in exactly the same place, size and shape; nothing is added, removed or moved except light. Keep the loose, broken Impressionist brushstrokes.

Only the lighting changes. The whole map lies under one shared, soft blue-violet moonlit night with a deep blue sky, a bright moon and stars, so the picture stays one harmonious painting; the warm lights are small glows inside that same night, never separate bright patches:
- Paris: gas lamps glow along the boulevards and quays, the station's glass roof is lit from inside, the garden café sparkles with small paper lanterns over the dancers, and their reflections tremble on the river in gold and orange dabs.
- Giverny: moonlight shimmers on the water lilies and the river in silver and violet; a few warm windows glow in Monet's house; the haystacks and poppies lie quiet in the moonlight.
- Japanese water garden: small stone lanterns glow softly by the water, the tea house has a warm light inside, the plum blossoms catch the moonlight.
- The road is softly lit along its length by small lamps, and a lamp stands beside each round plaza so that all three plazas lie in their own soft pool of light and stay clearly visible.

The round plazas stay empty: no fountain, water, figure or object on them. The flat grey margins on both sides (if any) continue the same night scenery. No text, letters, numbers, watermark, frames or borders.
