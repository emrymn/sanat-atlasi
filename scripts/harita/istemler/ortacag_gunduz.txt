The result is a tall vertical (1:3) bird's-eye storybook map of one art-historical period.

{{YERLESIM}}

Paint the whole picture edge to edge as one continuous landscape. Nothing of the flat sketch may remain: no blank paper background, no flat beige strip, no vignettes or framed scenes, no clouds separating the zones. The zones flow into each other.

The road is a real painted road whose surface changes with each zone (worn stone in Assisi, brick paving in Siena, a snowy track near the château), but its position and width never change.

Each of the {{N}} ovals becomes a small, round, empty paved plaza, flat and at ground level like the road (not a raised disc, step or pedestal). Nothing stands on a plaza: no animals, objects or people (a small statue will be placed there later). The period structure named for that level stands right beside its plaza.

The world is made of famous late medieval and Gothic paintings merged into one land. Each hidden detail stays next to its own level and appears only once. The whole picture shares one consistent light and a gentle bird's-eye perspective, with a touch of the period: the clear tempera colours of Giotto and Duccio frescoes, small gold-leaf accents, and the jewel-like detail of a Limbourg manuscript miniature. Use a light, bright palette with clear daylight; the picture must not look dark or varnished. Keep it calm and uncluttered: each hidden detail is one small scene, and at least half of the picture is quiet open space (sky, hills, fields, paved squares) with no figures or buildings. No crowds, no extra scenes, no extra buildings beyond those listed.

From bottom to top:
1 (lowest plaza): Assisi. Beside the plaza, the Basilica of Saint Francis with its arcaded terraces. Hidden nearby, on a rocky hillside path below a little hill town: the young Saint Francis with a halo giving his orange-red cloak to a poor knight, a brown horse with a red saddle cloth beside them (Giotto, Saint Francis Giving His Cloak to a Poor Knight).
2: Siena. Beside the plaza, the Palazzo Pubblico with the tall slender Torre del Mangia. Hidden nearby, inside an open arcaded chapel beside the square (seen through its arches, part of the town, not a framed picture): the Virgin in a deep blue mantle enthroned on a tall carved throne with the Child on her knee, three kneeling angels on each side of the throne (Duccio, Rucellai Madonna).
3: A fairy-tale French château with pointed blue roofs and white towers beside the plaza. Hidden nearby: a snowy farmyard with a little house whose front is open, peasants warming themselves by the fire, sheep in a pen (Limbourg brothers, Très Riches Heures, February).
Above the third plaza the road continues through open country with no more plazas. Hidden there: a richly dressed procession of the Magi with horses and gold-trimmed robes winding along the hills (Gentile da Fabriano, Adoration of the Magi). Far away near the top, the silhouette of a Gothic cathedral and a walled town.

The top and bottom edges fade into soft mist so the map can join the neighbouring periods. No text, letters, numbers or watermark. No frames or borders. No modern objects. Small figures may appear inside the hidden scenes, but no large characters stand on the road.
