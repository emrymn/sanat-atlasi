The result is a tall vertical (1:3) bird's-eye storybook map of one art-historical period: Impressionism (about 1860 to 1890) and the Japanese prints that inspired it, one single journey up the river Seine on a radiant summer day: from sunny Paris and Montmartre, along the riverbanks and poppy fields the Impressionists painted, to Monet's garden at Giverny and a Japanese-inspired water garden in blossom on the same river.

The picture has three zones from bottom to top, each about one third of its height, with one plaza in the middle of each: Paris on the Seine (bottom), Giverny on the Seine (middle) and a Japanese water garden on the Seine (top). Paint the whole picture edge to edge as one continuous, airy landscape with plenty of open space. One and the same river, the Seine, winds through the whole picture from bottom to top beside the road and ties all three zones together: it flows past the sunny Paris quays at the bottom, between willows, poplars, red poppy meadows and small sailing boats in the middle, and feeds the water-lily pond and the Japanese water garden at the top. The road follows the river; where it crosses it, it does so on a small bridge. Nothing of the flat sketch may remain: no blank paper background, no flat beige strip, no vignettes or framed scenes.

The road is a real painted road whose surface changes with each zone (sunlit Paris cobbles, a gravel garden path, raked pale gravel with stepping stones), but its position and width never change. It runs without any break from the bottom edge (entering at the bottom centre) to the top edge (leaving at the top centre); it never fades out, ends, climbs into the sky or disappears. There is no sea and no mountain anywhere. No rain anywhere.

Each of the {{N}} ovals becomes a small, round, empty paved plaza, flat and at ground level like the road (not a raised disc, step or pedestal). Nothing stands on a plaza: no fountain, basin, water, animals, objects or people (a small statue will be placed there later). The period structure named for that level stands right beside its plaza.

Most important: this is the joyful spirit of Impressionism: sunlight and colour. The whole map lies under one bright, warm summer sun from the upper left, with one continuous clear blue sky with a few white clouds and the same soft, coloured (violet and blue) shadows everywhere. Light sparkles and trembles on the water in many colours; leaves glitter; dappled sunlight falls through the trees. The palette is vivid and luminous: sky blues, fresh greens, poppy reds, sunflower yellows, lilac, rose and turquoise, with no grey, no gloom and no muddy brown. The whole map is painted as one Impressionist painting with loose, broken, flickering brushstrokes in the manner of Monet and Renoir; each zone only adds a gentle touch of its own artists, blending gradually into its neighbours:
- Paris (bottom): sun-dappled boulevards and cafés, as in Renoir, Manet and Caillebotte.
- Giverny (middle): Monet's bright dabs of colour, flowers, greens and pinks shimmering on water.
- Japanese water garden (top): blossoming plum trees and a hint of the clean outlines and elegant patterns of Japanese woodblock prints, still painted with the same sunny Impressionist brush.
No hard borders, no separate skies, no zone in its own frame.

The world is made of famous paintings merged into one land. Each hidden detail is a small scene painted directly into the landscape, never a framed picture, panel, canvas or rectangle pasted on the map, and appears only once. Keep each hidden scene small (no wider than about a third of the picture). At least half of the picture is quiet open space (sky, gardens, water, meadows, paved squares). No crowds beyond the small group in the first hidden scene, no extra scenes, no extra buildings beyond those listed. All figures are fully clothed.

From bottom to top:
1 (lowest plaza): Paris. Beside the plaza, the iron-and-glass train shed of the Gare Saint-Lazare with a little white steam, at a sunny Haussmann crossroads of cream-coloured buildings near the Seine quays. Hidden nearby, in a garden café under acacia trees: a small group of young Parisians in striped dresses and straw boater hats dancing and chatting at tables, spots of sunlight falling through the leaves onto their clothes, paper lanterns hanging above (Pierre-Auguste Renoir, Bal du moulin de la Galette).
2 (middle plaza): Giverny. Beside the plaza, Monet's long pink house with green shutters and a flower garden full of roses and nasturtiums. Hidden nearby: a green arched Japanese footbridge over a pond covered with water lilies, fed by the river, weeping willows around it, all glowing in the sun (Claude Monet, The Water Lily Pond).
3 (top plaza): a Japanese water garden on the river. Beside the plaza, a wooden tea house and a small red pagoda among pines and maples by the water. Hidden nearby: an old plum tree with a dark, twisting trunk and branches covered in white blossoms in the foreground, more blossoming plum trees and a low bamboo fence behind it with a few small figures in kimonos strolling, a warm rosy-red sky glowing above them (Hiroshige, Plum Park in Kameido). No writing, signs or cartouches.

The top edge fades into a bright summer sky and the bottom edge into sunny Paris paving so the map can join the neighbouring periods; the road reaches both edges. No text, letters, numbers, signs or watermark. No frames or borders. No modern objects. Small figures may appear inside the hidden scenes, but no large characters stand on the road.
