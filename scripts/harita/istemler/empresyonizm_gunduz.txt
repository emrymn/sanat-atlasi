The result is a tall vertical (1:3) bird's-eye storybook map of one art-historical period: Impressionism (about 1860 to 1890) and the Japanese prints that inspired it, a journey from rainy Paris through Monet's garden at Giverny to a Japanese garden.

{{YERLESIM}}

The picture has three zones from bottom to top, each about one third of its height, with one plaza in the middle of each: Paris (bottom), Giverny (middle) and a Japanese garden (top). Paint the whole picture edge to edge as one continuous, airy landscape with plenty of open space. Nothing of the flat sketch may remain: no blank paper background, no flat beige strip, no vignettes or framed scenes. The zones flow into each other.

The road is a real painted road whose surface changes with each zone (wet Paris cobbles, a gravel garden path, raked pale gravel with stepping stones), but its position and width never change. It runs without any break from the bottom edge (entering at the bottom centre) to the top edge (leaving at the top centre); it never fades out, ends, climbs into the sky or disappears. There is no sea anywhere and no mountain anywhere.

Each of the {{N}} ovals becomes a small, round, empty paved plaza, flat and at ground level like the road (not a raised disc, step or pedestal). Nothing stands on a plaza: no fountain, basin, water, animals, objects or people (a small statue will be placed there later). The period structure named for that level stands right beside its plaza.

Most important: the picture must read as ONE whole painting, not a patchwork. The whole map lies under one shared, bright summer-afternoon light from the upper left, with one continuous sky and the same soft shadows everywhere, and the colours are harmonised. Within that unity each zone is clearly recognisable by its own manner, and the manners blend gradually where the zones meet:
- Paris (bottom): Impressionist broken, flickering brushstrokes, soft grey-blue and lilac light on wet cobbles after the rain, as in Caillebotte, Manet and Degas.
- Giverny (middle): Monet's bright dabs of colour, flowers, greens and pinks shimmering on water.
- Japanese garden (top): the clean outlines, flat clear colour areas and elegant patterns of Japanese woodblock prints, still painted with soft Impressionist light.
No hard borders, no separate skies, no zone in its own frame.

The world is made of famous paintings merged into one land. Each hidden detail is a small scene painted directly into the landscape, never a framed picture, panel, canvas or rectangle pasted on the map, and appears only once. Keep each hidden scene small (no wider than about a third of the picture). At least half of the picture is quiet open space (sky, gardens, water, lawns, paved squares). No crowds, no extra scenes, no extra buildings beyond those listed. All figures are fully clothed.

From bottom to top:
1 (lowest plaza): Paris. Beside the plaza, the iron-and-glass train shed of the Gare Saint-Lazare with a little steam, at a Haussmann crossroads of cream-coloured buildings. Hidden nearby, on the wet cobbled square: a well-dressed couple under a large black umbrella walking towards us, a few other figures with umbrellas, a tall green street lamp, glistening paving stones (Gustave Caillebotte, Paris Street; Rainy Day).
2 (middle plaza): Giverny. Beside the plaza, Monet's long pink house with green shutters and a flower garden full of roses and nasturtiums. Hidden nearby: a green arched Japanese footbridge over a pond covered with water lilies, weeping willows around it (Claude Monet, The Water Lily Pond).
3 (top plaza): a Japanese garden. Beside the plaza, a wooden tea house and a small red pagoda among pines and maples. Hidden nearby: a long wooden bridge over a river in a sudden slanting downpour, a few small figures in straw hats and capes hurrying across it, a raft on the dark water (Hiroshige, Sudden Shower over Shin-Ōhashi Bridge).

The top edge fades into a soft pale sky and the bottom edge into Paris paving so the map can join the neighbouring periods; the road reaches both edges. No text, letters, numbers or watermark. No frames or borders. No modern objects. Small figures may appear inside the hidden scenes, but no large characters stand on the road.
