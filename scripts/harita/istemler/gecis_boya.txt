Image 1 is one tall picture made of two paintings of a continuous storybook map with an empty flat grey band between them. At the bottom is the top edge of the Renaissance map: green Italian hills, a hill town, a triumphal arch and a pale stone road coming up from below. At the top is the bottom edge of the Baroque map: a dark, golden, candle-lit Baroque world with St Peter's dome, a colonnade and the same pale stone road continuing upwards. Do not change the top and bottom paintings at all.

Paint only the empty grey band in the middle, as a real piece of landscape that joins the two paintings into one continuous journey through time, with no visible seam, border or line. The pale stone road coming up from the bottom continues without any break through the band and meets the road at the top exactly where it enters. Along the band the world slowly changes from the Renaissance to the Baroque: the bright, calm Tuscan daylight and green hills at the bottom gradually give way, as the road climbs, to the warm golden evening light, deeper shadows and dramatic sky of the Baroque at the top; the architecture along the road changes from simple Renaissance villas and cypresses to richer Baroque façades and statues. The light, colours and brushwork blend smoothly from the style of the bottom painting to the style of the top painting.

No plaza, no figures on the road, no frames, no text, letters, numbers or watermark.
