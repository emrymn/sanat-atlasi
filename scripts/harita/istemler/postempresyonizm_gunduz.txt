The result is a tall vertical (1:3) bird's-eye storybook map of one art-historical period: Post-Impressionism, Symbolism and Art Nouveau (about 1885 to 1910), a journey from Cézanne's Provence through Van Gogh's Arles and a dreamlike Symbolist lake to golden Vienna.

The picture has four zones from bottom to top, each about one quarter of its height, with one plaza in the middle of each: Provence (bottom), Arles, a Symbolist lake, and Vienna (top). Paint the whole picture edge to edge as one continuous, airy landscape with plenty of open space. Nothing of the flat sketch may remain: no blank paper background, no flat beige strip, no vignettes or framed scenes. The zones flow into each other.

The road is a real painted road whose surface changes with each zone (a dusty Provençal lane, an ochre country road, pale marble paving, a golden mosaic path), but its position and width never change. It runs without any break from the bottom edge (entering at the bottom centre) to the top edge (leaving at the top centre); it never fades out, ends, climbs into the sky or disappears. There is no sea anywhere.

Each of the {{N}} ovals becomes a small, round, empty paved plaza, flat and at ground level like the road (not a raised disc, step or pedestal). Nothing stands on a plaza: no fountain, basin, water, animals, objects or people (a small statue will be placed there later). The period structure named for that level stands right beside its plaza.

Most important: the picture must read as ONE whole painting, not a patchwork. The whole map lies under one shared, bright summer-afternoon light from the upper left, with one continuous sky and the same soft shadows everywhere, and the colours are harmonised. Within that unity each zone is clearly recognisable by the manner of its own artists, and the manners blend gradually where the zones meet:
- Provence (bottom): Cézanne's blocky, constructive planes of colour and Seurat's small dots of pure colour.
- Arles: Van Gogh's thick, swirling, rhythmic strokes, vivid yellows and blues.
- Symbolist lake: soft, dreamlike, mysterious tones of violet and teal, calm and still.
- Vienna (top): Klimt's shimmering gold leaf and decorative mosaic patterns.
No hard borders, no separate skies, no zone in its own frame, no band crossing the whole width.

The world is made of famous paintings merged into one land. Each hidden detail is a small scene painted directly into the landscape, never a framed picture, panel, canvas, block or rectangle pasted on the map, and appears only once. Keep each hidden scene small (no wider than about a third of the picture). At least half of the picture is quiet open space (sky, fields, water, lawns, paved squares). No crowds, no extra scenes, no extra buildings beyond those listed. All figures are fully clothed. Mont Sainte-Victoire appears only once and there are no other mountains.

From bottom to top:
1 (lowest plaza): Provence. Beside the plaza, Cézanne's ochre farmhouse (Jas de Bouffan) with chestnut trees, and Mont Sainte-Victoire rising pale blue-grey in the distance behind it. Hidden nearby, on a sunny riverside lawn: elegant figures in long dresses and top hats strolling and sitting, one lady with a parasol and a small monkey, all painted in small dots of pure colour (Georges Seurat, A Sunday on La Grande Jatte).
2: Arles. Beside the plaza, Van Gogh's Yellow House with green shutters on a small square, and a café terrace with a yellow awning. Hidden nearby, only in a small corner: a tiny village with a church spire under a tall dark flame-like cypress, above which a small patch of deep blue sky swirls with glowing stars and a crescent moon, like a vision; it stays small and does not cross the picture (Vincent van Gogh, The Starry Night).
3: a Symbolist lake. Beside the plaza, a mysterious white marble temple with columns among dark cypresses. Hidden nearby, on a dark still lake: a small rocky island of pale cliffs with tall dark cypresses and tomb doorways cut into the rock, a little boat with a white-draped standing figure approaching it (Arnold Böcklin, Isle of the Dead).
4 (top plaza): Vienna. Beside the plaza, the Vienna Secession building: a white cubic pavilion crowned with a dome of golden laurel leaves. Hidden nearby, on a flowery meadow: two small figures of a couple embracing, standing in the meadow itself, wrapped in robes covered with golden geometric and floral patterns, flowers at their feet (Gustav Klimt, The Kiss); they are figures in the landscape, not a golden panel.

The top edge fades into a softly golden sky and the bottom edge into Provençal fields so the map can join the neighbouring periods; the road reaches both edges. No text, letters, numbers or watermark. No frames or borders. No modern objects. Small figures may appear inside the hidden scenes, but no large characters stand on the road.
