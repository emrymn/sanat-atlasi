The result is a tall vertical (1:3) bird's-eye storybook map of one art-historical period: the 18th century, from the playful Rococo to cool Neoclassicism, a journey from a French pleasure garden through the English countryside up to the Panthéon in Paris.

The picture has three zones from bottom to top, each about one third of its height, with one plaza in the middle of each: a Rococo garden (bottom), the English countryside (middle) and Neoclassical Paris (top). Paint the whole picture edge to edge as one continuous landscape. Nothing of the flat sketch may remain: no blank paper background, no flat beige strip, no vignettes or framed scenes. The zones flow into each other.

The road is a real painted road whose surface changes with each zone (pale raked gravel in the Rococo garden, a sandy country lane in England, smooth pale stone paving in Paris), but its position and width never change. It runs without any break from the bottom edge (entering at the bottom centre) to the top edge (leaving at the top centre); it never fades out, ends or disappears.

Each of the {{N}} ovals becomes a small, round, empty paved plaza, flat and at ground level like the road (not a raised disc, step or pedestal). Nothing stands on a plaza: no fountain, basin, water, animals, objects or people (a small statue will be placed there later). The period structure named for that level stands right beside its plaza.

The style changes from bottom to top, as the century does:
- Bottom third (Rococo): the frothy, playful manner of Fragonard and Watteau: soft pastel pinks, powder blues, mint greens and creamy whites, feathery trees, rose bushes and garlands, light dappled sunshine, delicate curling ornament.
- Middle third (England): Gainsborough's feathery, airy brushwork: rolling green fields, golden wheat, tall oaks, a soft blue sky with silvery clouds.
- Top third (Neoclassicism): the crisp, clear, cool manner of Jacques-Louis David: clean lines, white marble, sharp light and shadow, calm blue sky, sober reds and blues.
The three styles blend softly where the zones meet; no hard borders.

Use a light, luminous and lively palette with varied, clear colours; the picture must not look dark, varnished or uniformly brown, and must not be neon. The world is made of famous paintings merged into one land. Each hidden detail is a small scene painted directly into the landscape, never a framed picture, panel, canvas or rectangle pasted on the map, and appears only once. Keep each hidden scene small (no wider than about a third of the picture). At least half of the picture is quiet open space (sky, gardens, fields, lawns, paved squares). No crowds, no extra scenes, no extra buildings beyond those listed. All figures are fully clothed.

From bottom to top:
1 (lowest plaza): a Rococo pleasure garden. Beside the plaza, a small pastel Rococo pavilion with white and gold ornament, like the Amalienburg, among flower beds and groves. Hidden nearby, in a leafy grove: a young woman in a billowing pink silk dress on a swing hanging from a tree, one small pink slipper flying through the air, a man in the bushes below and an older man pushing the swing from behind, a stone statue of Cupid watching (Fragonard, The Swing).
2 (middle plaza): the English countryside. Beside the plaza, a Palladian country house with a white columned portico; the roofs and spires of London far away. Hidden nearby, under a great oak at the edge of a wheat field: a young couple, the man standing with a gun and a dog, the woman in a pale blue silk dress sitting on a garden bench, golden wheat sheaves and wide green fields behind them (Gainsborough, Mr and Mrs Andrews).
3 (top plaza): Neoclassical Paris. Beside the plaza, the Panthéon with its great white columned portico and dome. Hidden nearby, in a stern hall of Doric columns and arches open to the square: three Roman brothers in red, white and blue tunics and helmets raising their right arms in an oath towards three swords held up by their father, the grieving women seated to one side (Jacques-Louis David, The Oath of the Horatii).

The top edge fades into a clear pale sky and the bottom edge into soft garden greenery so the map can join the neighbouring periods; the road reaches both edges. No text, letters, numbers or watermark. No frames or borders. No modern objects. Small figures may appear inside the hidden scenes, but no large characters stand on the road.
