The result is a tall vertical (1:3) bird's-eye storybook map of one art-historical period: Modern art (about 1890 to 1960), a journey from Munch's fjord through a Cubist Paris café, a Dada cabaret and a Surrealist dream desert to a white modern museum.

The picture has five zones from bottom to top, each about one fifth of its height, with one plaza in the middle of each: an Expressionist fjord (bottom), a Cubist Paris café, a Dada cabaret in Zurich, a Surrealist dream desert, and a modern museum (top). Paint the whole picture edge to edge as one continuous, airy landscape with plenty of open space. Nothing of the flat sketch may remain: no blank paper background, no flat beige strip, no vignettes or framed scenes. The zones flow into each other.

The road is a real painted road whose surface changes with each zone (wooden planks by the fjord, Paris paving broken into Cubist facets, a patchwork of coloured paper shapes, smooth pale desert sand, white stone), but its position and width never change. It runs without any break from the bottom edge (entering at the bottom centre) to the top edge (leaving at the top centre); it never fades out, ends, climbs into the sky or disappears. There is no sea anywhere (the fjord is a narrow inlet of water beside the road, not an open sea, and does not touch the bottom edge).

Each of the {{N}} ovals becomes a small, round, empty paved plaza, flat and at ground level like the road (not a raised disc, step or pedestal). Nothing stands on a plaza: no fountain, basin, water, animals, objects or people (a small statue will be placed there later). The period structure named for that level stands right beside its plaza.

Most important: the picture must read as ONE whole painting, not a patchwork. The whole map lies under one shared, bright, clear afternoon light from the upper left, with one continuous sky and the same crisp shadows everywhere. The mood is bright, joyful and full of colour: the pure, saturated colours of Kandinsky and Franz Marc (cobalt blue, vermilion, lemon yellow, emerald), no grey, no gloom, no muddy brown. Within that unity each zone is clearly recognisable by the manner of its own artists, and the manners blend gradually where the zones meet:
- Expressionist fjord (bottom): bold, swirling, expressive brushstrokes and strong colours, as in Munch, Kirchner, Kandinsky and Marc.
- Cubist café: forms broken into geometric facets and planes seen from several angles, as in Picasso and Braque, but in warm, lively colours.
- Dada cabaret: playful collage of cut paper shapes, stencilled patterns and found objects, as in Arp, Höch and Duchamp.
- Surrealist desert: smooth, precise, dreamlike clarity with long shadows and a vast calm sky, as in Dalí, Magritte and Miró.
- Modern museum (top): clean white architecture, bright daylight, open lawns.
No hard borders, no separate skies, no zone in its own frame, no band crossing the whole width.

The world is made of famous works merged into one land. Each hidden detail is a small scene painted directly into the landscape, never a framed picture, panel, canvas or rectangle pasted on the map, and appears only once. Keep each hidden scene small (no wider than about a third of the picture). At least half of the picture is quiet open space (sky, water, meadows, sand, lawns, paved squares). No crowds, no extra scenes, no extra buildings beyond those listed. All figures are fully clothed. No text, letters or numbers anywhere, including on signs, posters and collages.

From bottom to top:
1 (lowest plaza): an Expressionist fjord. Beside the plaza, a long wooden bridge with a railing along a narrow fjord inlet and a few brightly coloured Norwegian wooden houses. Hidden nearby, on the bridge: a small pale figure holding its hands to its face under a swirling blood-orange and yellow sky reflected in the blue water, two small figures further along the railing (Edvard Munch, The Scream); and on a green hill above, a few large bright blue horses grazing (Franz Marc, The Large Blue Horses).
2: a Cubist Paris café. Beside the plaza, a Paris café with a terrace, tables and chairs, its walls, windows and awning broken into Cubist facets. Hidden nearby, on the café terrace: three musicians made of flat, brightly coloured geometric shapes playing their instruments, a harlequin in a diamond-patterned costume with a guitar, a white Pierrot with a clarinet and a figure in a brown monk's robe with sheet music (Pablo Picasso, Three Musicians).
3 (middle plaza): Dada in Zurich. Beside the plaza, the small Cabaret Voltaire, its façade covered with a playful collage of coloured paper shapes and patterns (no writing). Hidden nearby, on a little square: a large bicycle wheel mounted upside down on a white wooden kitchen stool (Marcel Duchamp, Bicycle Wheel).
4: a Surrealist dream desert. Beside the plaza, nothing but the vast smooth desert, a distant golden rocky cape and a calm sky with floating white clouds. Hidden nearby: soft melting pocket watches draped over the branch of a bare dead tree and over the edge of a plain brown block, long shadows on the sand (Salvador Dalí, The Persistence of Memory); and, floating a little above the sand, a man's black bowler hat with a green apple hovering in front of it (René Magritte).
5 (top plaza): a modern museum. Beside the plaza, a clean, white, modern museum building with large glass windows and a wide lawn, the end of the journey. Hidden nearby, seen through one large window: a dark violet room with a thin gold line frame drawn like a glass box around an empty purple armchair (in the manner of Francis Bacon).

The top edge fades into a bright clear sky and the bottom edge into green hills and the wooden road so the map can join the neighbouring periods; the road reaches both edges. No text, letters, numbers or watermark. No frames or borders. No modern vehicles. Small figures may appear inside the hidden scenes, but no large characters stand on the road.
