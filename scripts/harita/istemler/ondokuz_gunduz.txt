The result is a tall vertical (1:3) bird's-eye storybook map of one art-historical period: Impressionism and after (about 1860 to 1910), a journey from rainy Paris through Monet's garden, a Japanese garden, Provence and Arles to a dreamlike Symbolist island and golden Vienna.

The picture has seven zones from bottom to top, each about one seventh of its height, with one plaza in the middle of each: Paris, Giverny, a Japanese garden, Provence, Arles, a Symbolist lake, and Vienna (top). Paint the whole picture edge to edge as one continuous landscape. Nothing of the flat sketch may remain: no blank paper background, no flat beige strip, no vignettes or framed scenes. The zones flow into each other.

The road is a real painted road whose surface changes with each zone (wet Paris cobbles, a gravel garden path, raked pale gravel with stepping stones, a dusty Provençal lane, an ochre country road, pale marble paving, a golden mosaic path), but its position and width never change. It runs without any break from the bottom edge (entering at the bottom centre) to the top edge (leaving at the top centre); it never fades out, ends, climbs into the sky or disappears. There is no sea anywhere.

Each of the {{N}} ovals becomes a small, round, empty paved plaza, flat and at ground level like the road (not a raised disc, step or pedestal). Nothing stands on a plaza: no fountain, basin, water, animals, objects or people (a small statue will be placed there later). The period structure named for that level stands right beside its plaza.

Most important: the picture must read as ONE whole painting, not a patchwork. The whole map lies under one shared, bright summer-afternoon light from the upper left, with one continuous sky and the same soft shadows everywhere, and the colours are harmonised across the zones. Within that unity each zone is clearly recognisable by the brushwork and manner of its own artists, and these manners blend gradually into one another where the zones meet:
- Paris (bottom): Impressionist broken, flickering brushstrokes, soft grey-blue and lilac light on wet cobbles.
- Giverny: Monet's bright dabs of colour, flowers, greens and pinks shimmering on water.
- Japanese garden: the clean outlines, flat clear colour areas and elegant patterns of Japanese woodblock prints.
- Provence: Cézanne's blocky, constructive planes and Seurat's small dots of pure colour.
- Arles: Van Gogh's thick, swirling, rhythmic strokes, vivid yellows and blues.
- Symbolist lake: soft, dreamlike, mysterious tones of violet and teal, calm and still.
- Vienna (top): Klimt's shimmering gold leaf and decorative mosaic patterns.
No hard borders, no separate skies, no zone in its own frame.

The world is made of famous paintings merged into one land. Each hidden detail is a small scene painted directly into the landscape, never a framed picture, panel, canvas or rectangle pasted on the map, and appears only once. Keep each hidden scene small (no wider than about a third of the picture). At least half of the picture is quiet open space (sky, gardens, water, fields, lawns, paved squares). No crowds, no extra scenes, no extra buildings beyond those listed. All figures are fully clothed. No mountains anywhere except Mont Sainte-Victoire in Provence.

From bottom to top:
1 (lowest plaza): Paris. Beside the plaza, the iron-and-glass train shed of the Gare Saint-Lazare with a little steam, at a Haussmann crossroads of cream-coloured buildings. Hidden nearby, on the wet cobbled square: a well-dressed couple under a large black umbrella walking towards us, a few other figures with umbrellas, a tall green street lamp, glistening paving stones (Gustave Caillebotte, Paris Street; Rainy Day).
2: Giverny. Beside the plaza, Monet's long pink house with green shutters and a flower garden full of roses and nasturtiums. Hidden nearby: a green arched Japanese footbridge over a pond covered with water lilies, weeping willows around it (Claude Monet, The Water Lily Pond).
3: a Japanese garden. Beside the plaza, a wooden tea house and a small red pagoda among pines and maples. No mountain. Hidden nearby: a long wooden bridge over a river in a sudden slanting downpour, a few small figures in straw hats and capes hurrying across it, a raft on the dark water (Hiroshige, Sudden Shower over Shin-Ōhashi Bridge).
4 (middle plaza): Provence. Beside the plaza, Cézanne's ochre farmhouse (Jas de Bouffan) with chestnut trees, and Mont Sainte-Victoire rising pale blue-grey behind it. Hidden nearby, on a sunny riverside lawn: elegant figures in long dresses and top hats strolling and sitting, one lady with a parasol and a small monkey, all painted in small dots of pure colour (Georges Seurat, A Sunday on La Grande Jatte).
5: Arles. Beside the plaza, Van Gogh's Yellow House with green shutters and a café terrace with a yellow awning. Hidden nearby: a small village with a church spire nestled under a tall dark flame-like cypress, above which a patch of deep blue sky swirls with glowing stars and a crescent moon, as in a vision (Vincent van Gogh, The Starry Night).
6: a Symbolist lake. Beside the plaza, a mysterious white marble temple with columns among dark cypresses. Hidden nearby, on a dark still lake: a small rocky island of pale cliffs with tall dark cypresses and tomb doorways cut into the rock, a little boat with a white-draped standing figure approaching it (Arnold Böcklin, Isle of the Dead).
7 (top plaza): Vienna. Beside the plaza, the Vienna Secession building: a white cubic pavilion crowned with a dome of golden laurel leaves. Hidden nearby, on a flowery meadow: a couple embracing, wrapped in robes covered with golden geometric and floral patterns, against a shimmering gold background (Gustav Klimt, The Kiss).

The top edge fades into a softly golden sky and the bottom edge into Paris paving so the map can join the neighbouring periods; the road reaches both edges. No text, letters, numbers or watermark. No frames or borders. No modern objects. Small figures may appear inside the hidden scenes, but no large characters stand on the road.
