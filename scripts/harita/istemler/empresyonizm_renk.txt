Image 1 is a finished bird's-eye map of Impressionism: one river winding from sunny Paris (bottom: the Gare Saint-Lazare, a garden café with dancers, quays and sailing boats) through Giverny (middle: Monet's pink house, the water-lily pond with a green Japanese footbridge) to a Japanese water garden with blossoming plum trees, a tea house and a red pagoda (top). Repaint exactly the same picture with only two changes. Keep the composition identical: the road, all three round plazas, every building, figure, tree, boat, bridge and the river stay in exactly the same place, size and shape.

Change 1, the light: the whole map is now bathed in the warm golden light of a late summer afternoon, as in Monet's paintings of haystacks and sunsets on the Seine. The many greens of lawns, trees and banks turn into warm golden, amber, orange and ochre tones with only touches of soft green left; the poplars and willows glow gold in the low sun; the river and pond shimmer in rose, lilac, gold and blue reflections; the sky becomes a warm golden-peach afternoon sky with a little lilac. Shadows are soft violet and blue. It stays luminous and joyful, painted with the same loose, broken Impressionist brushstrokes; never dull, brown or dark.

Change 2: there are two green arched Japanese footbridges over the water-lily pond in the middle. Keep the upper one, next to Monet's pink house. Remove the lower one completely and repaint its place as the same pond with water lilies and reflections.

The round plazas stay empty: no fountain, water, figure or object on them. The flat grey margins on both sides (if any) continue the same scenery. No text, letters, numbers, watermark, frames or borders.
